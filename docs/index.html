Sacred Classical Philosophy

The Authentic Eight Limbs of Ashtanga Yoga

Codified by Sage Patañjali in the classical Yoga Sutras, Ashtanga (literally ashta = eight, anga = limbs) is a holistic, progressive path of spiritual awakening, ethical living, and mental stillness.

यमनियमासनप्राणायामप्रत्याहारधारणाध्यानसमाधयोऽष्टावङ्गानि ॥ २.२९ ॥
yama-niyama-āsana-prāṇāyāma-pratyāhāra-dhāraṇā-dhyāna-samādhayo ’ṣṭāv aṅgāni
"The eight limbs of yoga are: moral restraints (Yama), internal observances (Niyama), physical postures (Asana), breath control (Pranayama), sensory withdrawal (Pratyahara), concentration (Dharana), meditation (Dhyana), and meditative absorption (Samadhi)."
Patañjali’s Yoga Sutras • Sadhana Pada 2.29
Limb 01 ⚖️
यम

Yama

Universal Ethical Disciplines

The foundational moral guidelines governing our relationship with society and the world. Yamas establish the inner tranquility and clear conscience essential for deep contemplative practice.

The 5 Restraints: • Ahimsa (Non-violence)
• Satya (Truthfulness)
• Asteya (Non-stealing)
• Brahmacharya (Energy moderation)
• Aparigraha (Non-possessiveness)
Limb 02 🌿
नियम

Niyama

Personal Self-Purification

Internal observances and self-disciplines that purify personal habits, emotional balance, and devotion to nurture spiritual growth.

The 5 Observances: • Saucha (Purity)
• Santosha (Contentment)
• Tapas (Spiritual heat & discipline)
• Svadhyaya (Self-study)
• Ishvara Pranidhana (Surrender)
Limb 03 🧘
आसन

Asana

Posture & Steady Seat

Defined in Yoga Sutra 2.46 as “Sthira Sukham Asanam” — posture must be both steady and joyful. In Ashtanga Yoga, progressive physical series (Yoga Chikitsa) detoxify tissues, strengthen muscles, and align the spine.

Ashtanga Embodiment: Dynamic breath-synchronized vinyasas cleanse internal organs, build endurance, and cultivate lightness (laghu).
Limb 04 💨
प्राणायाम

Pranayama

Breath & Life Force Expansion

The conscious regulation, expansion, and mastery of the vital life force (prana). In Ashtanga practice, deep victorious Ujjayi breathing maintains internal furnace heat and calms agitation.

Pranayama Pillars: • Puraka (Conscious Inhalation)
• Rechaka (Steady Exhalation)
• Kumbhaka (Mindful Retention)
Limb 05 🐢
प्रत्याहार

Pratyahara

Sensory Withdrawal

Withdrawing the senses from external attractions, like a tortoise drawing its limbs into its shell. In the asana practice, fixing the gaze (Drishti) turns sight inward.

The Bridge: Connects outer physical sadhana (Bahiranga) with inner contemplative sadhana (Antaranga).
Limb 06 🎯
धारणा

Dharana

Single-Pointed Concentration

Fixing mental awareness unconditionally upon a single point or object—such as the sound of the Ujjayi breath, the navel, or a sacred mantra.

Mental Training: Trains the monkey mind to cease flitting between past memories and future anxieties.
Limb 07 🌊
ध्यान

Dhyana

Continuous Meditative Absorption

When concentration (Dharana) becomes unbroken and continuous, like an uninterrupted stream of oil poured from one vessel into another, it transforms into true meditation (Dhyana).

Effortless Flow: The practitioner experiences deep tranquility and communion with the present moment.
Limb 08 ✨
समाधि

Samadhi

Spiritual Liberation & Pure Awareness

The supreme pinnacle of yoga sadhana. The illusion of separateness dissolves; the observer, the process of observing, and the observed merge into unified consciousness, peace, and bliss (Kaivalya).

The Goal of Yoga: Unconditioned spiritual freedom, timeless stillness, and self-realization.
The Sacred Core of Physical Practice

The Tristana Method: Three Pillars of Transformation

In Ashtanga Yoga, asana is never mere gymnastics. Posture is animated through the Tristana method—the simultaneous mastery of three sacred anchors:

🌬️

1. Ujjayi Pranayama (Breath)

Resonant throat-friction breathing warms the blood, clears toxins, and provides an auditory metronome for the moving mind.

🔒

2. Bandhas (Energy Locks)

Mula Bandha (pelvic root lock) and Uddiyana Bandha (lower abdominal lock) channel prana upward, producing lightness and spine stability.

🎯

3. Drishti (Focal Gaze)

Designated gazing points (nose tip, third eye, navel, hands, toes, sky) steady the optic nerve so the mind stops wandering outward.

The Classical Blueprint

The Ashtanga Primary Series — Yoga Chikitsa

The Primary Series of Ashtanga Yoga is traditionally named Yoga Chikitsa, meaning "Yoga Therapy" or purification. It is designed to cleanse internal organs, realign the spine, and build robust strength, stamina, and flexibility.

~10–20 min

☀️ Sun Salutations Only

Surya Namaskara A (×5) and Surya Namaskara B (×5). Awakens spinal articulation, builds internal heat, and establishes rhythmic Ujjayi breathing. Ideal for busy mornings or warmups.

Level: All Levels • Focus: Warmup & Breath Sync
~20–30 min

🌸 Finishing Sequence Only

Gentle backbends, shoulderstands (Salamba Sarvangasana), headstand (Sirsasana), meditative mudras, and final Savasana. Balances the endocrine system and calms the nervous system.

Level: Restorative • Focus: Inversions & Meditation
~45–60 min

🌿 Quarter Primary Series

Sun Salutations A & B, the full Standing Sequence for stability and balance (Padangusthasana through Virabhadrasana B), transitioning directly to the Finishing Sequence.

Level: Beginner to Intermediate • Focus: Standing Strength
~75–80 min

🌊 Half Primary Series

Sun Salutations, standing poses, and seated postures up to Navasana (Boat Pose), followed by finishing inversions. The traditional milestone to develop core power and open hips.

Level: Intermediate • Focus: Core & Forward Folds
~90–120 min

🧘 Full Primary Series — Complete Yoga Chikitsa

The complete classical sequence: Surya Namaskara A & B, full Standing sequence, all 24+ seated asanas (through Setu Bandhasana), closing backbends, complete inversions, and Savasana. Cleanses the nervous system, eliminates physical blockages, and cultivates unshakable mental focus.

Level: Experienced • Focus: Complete Primary Series

Sacred Rhythm & Moving Meditation

What Is the Ashtanga Vinyasa Count?

In Ashtanga Yoga, vinyasa does not mean a casual flow; it refers to the exact mathematical coordination of one breath with one physical movement. Postures are entered and exited following a strict classical Sanskrit count.

By counting each movement in traditional Sanskrit, your mind is shielded from mundane thought and internal chatter. The counting system synchronizes the breath with the nervous system, turning the physical asana sequence into an unbroken prayer in motion.

Frequently Asked Questions About Ashtanga & The App

Everything you need to know about traditional Ashtanga practice, Sanskrit counts, Mysore guidance, and the app's v3 progress tools, free preview, and offline features.

What makes Ashtanga Yoga different from other yoga styles?

Unlike generic Vinyasa or Hatha flow classes that change poses every session, Ashtanga Yoga follows a strict, progressive sequential order codified by Sri K. Pattabhi Jois. Every movement is synchronized with a specific inhalation or exhalation (Vinyasa), locked with internal muscular seals (Bandhas), and anchored by a designated focal gaze (Drishti).

Because the sequence remains constant, you do not need to think about what posture comes next. The physical sequence becomes a mirror of your mental state, converting your daily sadhana into a true moving meditation.

What is the Ashtanga Vinyasa count and why is it counted in Sanskrit?

The Vinyasa count is the classical mathematical framework of Ashtanga Yoga. Each transition into and out of a posture is numbered in classical Sanskrit: Ekam (1 - Inhale), Dve (2 - Exhale), Trīṇi (3 - Inhale), Catvāri (4 - Exhale), and so on.

Counting in Sanskrit honors the authentic yogic lineage, prevents idle mind-wandering, and establishes an unbroken energetic rhythm across your entire practice.

How does My Ashtanga Yoga Practice support Mysore-style self-practice?

Traditional Mysore-style practice requires keeping your eyes on your drishti (gazing point)—not looking up at an iPad or smartphone screen. Watching videos while in an inversion or deep forward fold strains your neck and breaks pratyahara (sensory withdrawal).

My Ashtanga Yoga Practice is engineered for screen-free guidance: gentle singing-bowl chimes, Sanskrit vinyasa audio counts, and breath pacing allow you to set your phone down and practice with pure auditory flow.

How does the Progress calendar and practice log work?

Finish a session with Done or let the sequence reach its end to save it automatically. The Progress tab shows a monthly calendar with daily active practice totals and duration badges. Browse previous months or tap Today to return to the current month.

The practice log groups sessions by day, with the newest day first. Each entry includes the sequence, duration, and completion time. Pauses are excluded. History stays on your device and remains available after the free preview ends.

Which features work offline?

Pose text, timers, bundled chants, and recorded breath counts work offline. Online voice guidance requires internet, while device speech fallback depends on installed voices. Purchases, restoration, and online subscription verification also require internet. No account signup is required.

How do I customize the audio-guided practice to fit my pace?

Tap the blue gear icon on any tab to open Settings, then find Customize Audio Guidance. With Pro, use the minus and plus controls beside Breaths Per Minute to choose a cadence from 4 to 14 BPM. Lower BPM gives you more time for each breath cycle; higher BPM makes the guidance faster. The default is 9 BPM, about 6.7 seconds per complete breath cycle.

You can also toggle Sanskrit Count, adjust Sun Salutations Repetitions (3 or 5 rounds), and choose whether to include Full Vinyasa Flow between postures. If you open Settings during practice, your session pauses. Return to Practice and tap Play when you are ready to continue.

How does health tracking work with Apple Health and Google Health Connect?

Your practice history is saved on your device automatically. Separately, you can choose to save a completed session from the practice summary to Apple Health on iOS or Google Health Connect on Android as a Yoga workout, with your permission.
